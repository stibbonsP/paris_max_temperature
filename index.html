Our approach

How to estimate probabilities of future temperature records?

  • We generate a random process mimicking the behavior of future daily temperatures
  • We deduce probability density functions for observables of interests, for instance the probability to reach a given temperature threshold

↓ Why not rely on climate-models ? ↓

  • The standard approach is to rely on Global Climate Models simulations
  • These can be downscaled to the regional scale
  • We can then look for extreme events in the resulting simulation

Why are we looking for an alternative?

  • Each simulation can take several months on powerful computers. This is a lengthy and expensive process.
  • We may need many simulations to infer density probability functions.
  • Parametrizing, running and interpreting these simulations requires sharp expertise in climate models.

The data-driven approach we explore is much quicker and cheaper.

It easily provides probability density functions instead of individual realizations.

To build synthetic temperature data, we combine three components:

  • A trend representing the impact of global warming
  • A seasonal pattern
  • Random fluctuations

First we need a fit for global warming

Is it realistic to assume linear warming until the end of the century?

  • This scenario lies in-between the "intermediate" and "high" scenarios of the IPCC
  • The "intermediate" scenario feels quite optimistic given the current GHG emission reduction rate

The next step is to fit the warming rate in Paris compared to global warming

  • We perform the linear regression: $$ \min_{c,\ T_0} \left(\mathrm{Paris\ temperature} - c \left(\mathrm{Global\ Warming}\right) - T_0 \right)^2 $$
  • We find \(c \approx \) 2.8, \(T_0 \approx 14.4\)°C

  • We perform the linear regression: $$ \min_{c,\ T_0} \left(\mathrm{Paris\ temperature} - c \left(\mathrm{Global\ Warming}\right) - T_0 \right)^2 $$

The factor 2.8 we fitted is surprisingly high.

To challenge our result, we repeat this fit with different data: the ERA5 temperatures for many locations in France.

  • On average, warming is twice faster in France than the world average, with significant geographic variations
  • Around Paris, we obtain a factor close to 2

So, which value should we trust between 2 and 2.8?

  • Large cities warm faster than their neighborhing countryside, by a factor \(\approx 1.3\) (Liu, Z., Zhan, W., Bechtel, B. et al)
  • The methodology used to build the ERA5 data makes it rather blind to the Urban Heat Island effect, which is responsible for this increased warming rate

$$ 2 \times 1.3 = 2.6 $$

  • The Urban Heat Island effect effect is particularly strong in Paris

$$ 2 \times \left(1.3 + \mathrm{Paris\ bonus}\right) \approx 2.8? $$

All in all, this makes the factor 2.8 rather plausible

The seasonal pattern

This is simply modeled by a combination of sinusoidal functions

We fit the seasonal pattern with two cosine functions (first two Fourier modes) of periods 1 year and 6 months

  • We perform the linear regression: $$ \min_{A_1,\ A_2,\ t_1,\ t_2} \left(\tilde{T} - A_1 \cos\left(\frac{t-t_1}{1\ \mathrm{year}}\right) - A_2 \cos\left(\frac{t-t_2}{6\ \mathrm{months}}\right) \right)^2 $$ where \(\tilde{T}\) is the Paris temperature minus the previously fitted global warming
  • We find \(A_1 \approx 9.4\)°C and \(A_2 \approx 0.9\)°C
